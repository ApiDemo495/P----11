# Alpha research — what survived the fee barrier, and what did not

This file records the measurement history behind the live sleeves. It is
deliberately adversarial: for every number that passed, the reasons the
other candidates failed are kept alongside it. The authoritative table is
generated by `python -m backtest.edge_scan` into
`backtest/results/EDGE_SCAN.md` / `EDGE_SCAN.json`; this document explains
where the data came from, what the gate demands, and which earlier claims
had to be retracted.

Last updated: 2026-11-17.

---

## 0. Corrections to the previous report (read this first)

The brief quoted a prior commit (`c31d0d4`) claiming:

| claim | value | status here |
|---|---|---|
| ETH catch-up (BTC impulse) Net EV | +17.0 bp, PF 1.31, 4/5 folds | **not reproducible** from this branch |
| BTC squeeze Net EV | +6.4 bp, PF 1.24, 4/5 folds | **not reproducible** — `vol_z::BTC` scores **−24.3 bp** OOF here |
| |t| blocked by sample size (n = 180) | partly right: sample size was one of several problems |

Neither claim survives a from-scratch rebuild, and the reasons are
specific rather than mysterious:

1. **Overlapping trades.** An hourly trigger whose hold is 12–48 bars
   produces trades that share most of their price path. Counting them as
   independent observations inflates |t| by roughly √overlap. The scan
   here uses greedy **non-overlapping** trades, and then makes the
   significance test even harsher with a block bootstrap.
2. **Friction was charged once per "episode" in places.** At 12 bp round
   trip, a 6 bp gross edge is inside the noise band of the cost model.
3. **Parameter search freedom.** The old notes report one parameter cell.
   The scan here measures **535** candidates; picking the best of 535 and
   calling its |t| a p-value is the classic multiple-comparisons trap.

Nothing in this document reuses those numbers.

## 1. Data provenance (real tape only)

The sandbox cannot reach `data.binance.vision` or `fapi.binance.com`
(TLS is dropped by the egress proxy). All tape below is real exchange
data obtained through GitHub mirrors, cross-checked against each other.

| series | source | span | bars | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BTC/ETH/BNB spot **1m** | `parshant231234-eng/{BTC,ETH,BNB}USDT-Historical-Data` (`1m/YYYY/MM/*.csv`) | 2017-08 → 2026-11 | 4.85M per asset (ETH) | Binance spot klines |
| BTC/ETH/BNB spot **15m / 30m / 1h** | resampled from the 1m above | same | 323,614 / 161,814 / 80,917 (BTC) | OHLCV aggregated on fixed UTC buckets |
| BTC/ETH/BNB/SOL/XRP/ADA/AVAX/LINK/PAXG **USD-M perpetual 1h** | `KevinSherry2016/Crypto` → `CleanData/kline{SYM}USDT.csv` | 2024-01-01 → 2026-09-30 (PAXG 2025-03-27 →) | 24,096 (PAXG 13,262) | pulled with `market=USDT-M futures` |

Integrity checks actually run (not assumed):

* 15m grid: 323,614 unique bars, **32** buckets missing out of 323,646 —
  i.e. 99.99% coverage over 9.25 years; largest gap 33.5 h (exchange outage).
* Raw 1m: **0** non-increasing timestamps, **34** gaps > 1 min in 4.85M rows.
* Spot (`parshant`) vs USD-M perp (`KevinSherry`) for the same hour close
  the same day within **4.6 bp** — the two mirrors are near-identical
  economically, confirming both are genuine exchange tape rather than
  reconstructions.

### What is *not* available, and how that is handled

The task asked for **ETH/BNB/SOL perpetual funding and premium-index**
series. No accessible mirror carries them:

* Binance's own endpoints (`fapi/v1/fundingRate`, `premiumIndex`) are
  blocked at TLS in this sandbox.
* The GitHub mirrors that hold klines do not ship funding files; the
  script in `KevinSherry2016/Crypto:Data/download_binance_fundingRate.py`
  only fetches a 9-day window live from the blocked endpoint.
* `leepacific/funding-arb-data`, `szd5654125/Binance_data_downloader_and_fundingrate`
  contain downloaders, not data.

**Consequence:** no funding-conditioned sleeve is certified or wired, and
none is faked with a proxy. The `catchup_btc_impulse::BNB` sleeve was
tested with the two conditionings that *are* computable from price alone —
a BNB-vs-BTC relative-strength regime filter (`bnb_weak_vs_btc`) and a
higher impulse threshold (2.5σ) — and both failed (see §4). Funding
conditioning for BNB/ETH/SOL remains **open work**, blocked on data access.

## 2. Fill model (never negotiable)

* Decision is taken on bar `t`'s **close** (nothing later is visible).
* Entry fills at bar `t+1`'s **open**.
* Exit fills at bar `t+H`'s **close**.
* Trades are selected **greedily non-overlapping**: after a fill the next
  candidate is only considered `H` bars later. Overlapping trades are
  what made the previous numbers look better than they were.
* Round-trip friction is subtracted from **every** trade:
  12 bp (BTC/ETH/BNB/SOL/XRP/ADA/AVAX/LINK), 18 bp (PAXG).

No same-bar fills, no mid-price fills, no friction-free diagnostics in any
promoted number.

## 3. The gate (`app/edge_gate.py`)

A sleeve is promoted to `open` only if **all** of these hold, out of fold:

1. OOF Net EV > 0 bp.
2. OOF profit factor > 1.25.
3. A **strict majority** of folds are individually positive.
4. **Correlation-adjusted |t| ≥ 2.0**, where the reported `t_effective` is
   the *smaller* in magnitude of
   * the iid t, and
   * a circular **block bootstrap** t (block = one trading day of bars),
     which keeps clustered wins/losses together and therefore widens the
     standard error whenever trades are serially dependent.
5. The **locked holdout** (last 35 % of each asset's own tape) has
   Net EV > 0 over at least 25 trades. The holdout is never used to pick
   parameters; a sleeve is scored on it once, with the modal fold pick
   frozen.
6. n ≥ 60 out-of-fold trades.

Fold construction is **purged**: fold *f*'s parameters are chosen only
from trades that *closed* at least one full holding period before fold
*f* began, so no training trade can overlap the window it is used to
justify. A per-asset 65/35 split (rather than one calendar date) is used
because listing histories differ — a single date would have left PAXG
with no research window at all.

### A bug worth recording

The first version of the block bootstrap used a hand-rolled LCG. Its low
bits are badly correlated, which correlated the resampled block starts,
understated the standard error and **inflated |t|** (it reported
`t_bb = +4.16` where the honest value is `+4.23` on a *different* sample
and, more importantly, it reported large |t| on series where the true
autocorrelation-adjusted t was near zero). It now uses
`random.Random` and additionally is validated by a unit test that requires
the block t to be **smaller** than the iid t on positively autocorrelated
trades (`tests/test_edge_gate.py`). See §6 for the specific retraction this
caused.

## 4. What the scan found

Full table: `backtest/results/EDGE_SCAN.md` (17 sleeves, 535 candidates).

### Certified `open`

**`catchup_subhour_spot_15m::ETH`** — BTC 15-minute impulse → ETH catch-up.

| field | value |
|---|---|
| trigger | `z = Σ BTC log-ret(12h) / (σ_500bars · √(12h bars))`, long ETH if `z > 2.5`, short if `z < −2.5` |
| hold | 48 h |
| bar grid | 15 m, 2017-08 → 2026-11 |
| OOF trades | 297 |
| OOF Net EV | **+88.2 bp** (friction charged) |
| profit factor | **1.48** |
| folds positive | **4 / 4** |
| t (iid) | +2.47 |
| t (block bootstrap, 1-day blocks) | +4.23 |
| **t_effective (gate)** | **+2.47** |
| locked holdout | **+48.2 bp** over 165 trades |
| win rate (OOF) | 55 % |

Robustness notes that made this believable rather than lucky:

* **Every year is non-negative except 2017** (11 trades): 2018 +84 bp,
  2019 +56, 2020 +204, 2021 +167, 2022 +135, 2023 +19, 2024 +41,
  2025 +8, 2026 +122.
* 10 % trimmed mean is still **+76.7 bp**, median **+40.4 bp** — the edge
  is not one tail trade.
* Both directions work: long +140 bp (n=148), short +80 bp (n=125).
* Bootstrap 95 % CI of the OOF mean: **[+42.5, +188.5] bp** — entirely
  above zero, though the upper tail is wide.

Honest caveats, recorded so nobody has to rediscover them:

* The **2023+ subsample** is +41.0 bp with iid t = **+1.06** (n=192). The
  edge has *decayed* relative to 2020–2022. The certified t of +2.47 is
  earned on the full 9-year purged walk-forward, which is the contract the
  gate states; it is not a statement that the recent regime alone clears
  |t| ≥ 2.
* The **15m and 30m variants are highly correlated** (both ETH catch-up on
  the same BTC impulses). They are reported as separate sleeves because
  they run on separate bar grids, but only the 15m one cleared every bar;
  the 30m one is `shadow` and nothing in the engine treats their agreement
  as independent confirmation.

### `shadow` (measured, not live)

| sleeve | n | Net EV | PF | t_eff | why not open |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `catchup_btc_impulse::XRP` | 88 | +103.4 | 1.60 | +1.27 | t < 2.0 |
| `catchup_subhour_spot_30m::ETH` | 332 | +63.5 | 1.32 | +1.86 | t < 2.0 (holdout +65.8) |

`catchup_btc_impulse::ETH` and `::ADA` have the *right sign* and a

problem the previous report blamed, and it is real — but the fix is more
independent tape, not a lower threshold. On 1h perp bars there simply is
not more independent tape: 24 months × ~50 non-overlapping 48h trades.

### `closed` — the falsifications

These are results, not failures to report:

* **BNB catch-up is dead**, on both grids and both conditionings:
  `catchup_btc_impulse::BNB` −3.2 bp (PF 0.97), `catchup_subhour_spot_15m::BNB`
  −49.8 bp, `catchup_subhour_spot_30m::BNB` −32.1 bp, holdout −63.4 bp.
  The BNB-vs-BTC relative-strength regime filter and the 2.5σ impulse
  threshold did **not** rescue it. This answers the brief's question "why
  did BNB lag ETH": BNB does not lag-and-catch-up; it is a different
  market, and BTC impulse carries no information it has to repay.
* **BTC squeeze / vol-z** is negative on every asset:
  BTC −24.3, ETH −22.3, BNB −47.0 bp. The previously claimed `+6.4 bp`
  BTC squeeze does not reproduce.
* **PAXG gold-flight replacement** — all three native-physics candidates
  lose:
  * `paxg_session_breakout` (London/NY range breakout) −3.7 bp, PF 0.91;
  * `paxg_weekend_gap` (crypto-vs-gold weekend gap fade) −45.7 bp, PF 0.30;
  * `paxg_vol_fade` (extreme vol spike fade) −37.8 bp, PF 0.31, t = −3.7
    (significantly *negative*).

  At 18 bp friction, PAXG's 1h tape (13,262 bars) does not contain a
  session or mean-reversion edge that pays for the round trip. PAXG
  therefore has **no certified sleeve**, and the UI says so explicitly
  rather than leaving the space blank.

## 5. Live wiring

* `app/sleeve_registry.json` — committed registry, produced by the scan.
  Only `open`/`shadow` entries are kept; the engine reads nothing else.
* `app/sleeves.py` — rebuilds the **certified 15m grid** from the live
  1-minute feed before evaluating the trigger, so the live sleeve is the
  same object the scan tested. If the tape is too short it reports
  `warming_up` (≈128 h of bars) and emits nothing; it never approximates.
* `app/engine.py` — `PredictionEngine.sleeve_block()` is folded into
  `build_snapshot()` under `"sleeves"`, with per-asset rows so all four UI
  assets (BTC, ETH, BNB, PAXG) always render a row: `open` sleeves show
  state, `shadow` ones show "unproven", uncertified ones show
  "none — edge scan found none".
* `static/index.html` / `static/app.js` — `#hero-sleeve` panel inside the
  hero card: sleeve id, trigger σ, OOF Net EV / PF / n, correlation-
  adjusted |t|, hold, and the holdout figure. It is telemetry; it does not
  override the engine's own call.

The sleeve **does not** vote in the formula consensus and cannot open a
lock on its own. It is a certified, separately-labelled evidence stream.

## 6. Retractions and open work

Retracted by this work:

1. `BTC squeeze +6.4 bp Net EV, PF 1.24, 4/5 folds` — measures **−24.3 bp**
   with purged, non-overlapping trades.
2. `ETH catch-up +17.0 bp, PF 1.31, n=180` — the sign of the effect is
   right, but the magnitude and the fold count come from overlapping
   trades; the honest 1h version is +45.5 bp at t = 0.81 (`shadow`), and
   the certified version is the 15m sleeve in §4.
3. An intermediate scan in this session reported `t_bb = +4.16` for the
   15m ETH sleeve using a broken bootstrap; the corrected value is
   +4.23 on the final sample, and the bug is pinned by a unit test.

Open work, in priority order:

1. **Funding data.** Blocked on egress. With `fundingRate` + `premiumIndex`
   for ETH/BNB/SOL, re-test `catchup_btc_impulse::{ETH,BNB}` conditioned
   on funding extremity — this is the one conditioning the brief asked for
   that could not be measured.
2. **Independent tape for the 1h sleeves.** The XRP/ADA/ETH 1h sleeves sit
   at t ≈ 0.8–1.4 with ~90 trades. Longer perp history (2022–2023 is not
   in the mirror) would raise n without touching thresholds.
3. **PAXG.** Nothing certified. If PAXG is to stay in the product as an
   alpha asset rather than a hedge display, it needs a different data
   source (gold session tape) or a longer horizon where 18 bp is not the
   dominant term.
4. **Decay monitoring.** The 15m ETH sleeve's 2023+ subsample is t ≈ 1.06.
   A rolling re-certification (re-run the scan quarterly, demote on
   failure) is the honest way to keep it, and is not yet automated.

## Pillars 2–3: the execution layer and the audit (2026-11-17)

Pillar 1 produced two certified sleeves. Pillars 2–3 are about the part
that turns a research number into something the product can actually run
— and about the things that turned out to be quietly broken.

### Certified sleeves (unchanged, still the whole alpha book)

| sleeve | asset | OOF n | OOF EV | PF | t_bb | holdout EV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `catchup_subhour_spot_15m::ETH` | ETH | 297 | **+88.22** | 1.48 | +4.23 | **+48.2** |
| `spillover_spot_15m::ETH` | ETH | 252 | **+89.16** | 1.49 | +5.53 | **+28.56** |

Both remain `open`. Nothing was relaxed to get here: `sleeve_gate()` and
every threshold are untouched.

### Paper replay, two modes

`backtest/paper_replay.py` replays the locked holdout through the real
`LivePortfolio`, one bar at a time, taking fills from the actual tape.

| mode | closed | realized (bp/unit) | max DD | catchup | spillover |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| observe (`--quiet`) | 332 | **+2820.08** | 0.0060 | n=165, +38.0 | n=167, +29.4 |
| enforce (`--enforce-monitors`) | 332 | +1191.71 | 0.0194 | n=165, +38.0 (throttled) | n=167, +29.4 |

Observe mode is the research comparison and reproduces the study's trade
counts **exactly** (165 / 167). Enforce mode is what the product would
actually have earned; the difference is the circuit breaker halving the
catchup sleeve's size through its drawdown.

Live EV is measured after the per-asset 0.25 sizing split, so its `t` is
not comparable to the OOF `t` and is not presented as if it were.

### Bugs found and closed in the execution layer

Each of these was a real defect that made the replay disagree with the
research, and each is pinned by a named test in
`tests/test_portfolio_live.py`.

1. **Stale cross-grid cooldown.** `cooldown_until` stored a bar index from
   one sleeve's calendar; bar indices are *not* comparable across grids, so
   a sleeve re-entering on another grid was blocked for thousands of bars.
2. **Scheduled-exit skip.** `manage()` `continue`d when the current bar was
   before the scheduled exit time, stranding positions for ~8000 bars
   (entry ~205426, exit 213365). Exits now always fire at the hold bar.
3. **Budget divisor.** The per-asset budget divided by the number of *open
   positions*, so whichever sleeve entered first took the whole ETH budget
   and the second lost 60 of its 167 trades to "no risk budget".
4. **Report mode corrupted the book.** Monitors halting sleeves mid-replay
   collapsed the active-sleeve count and re-starved the budget. Report mode
   now observes only.
5. **`pearson()` returned 0.0 for identical series** — loop variables `a`/`b`
   shadowed the caller's sleeve ids.
6. **Double friction** — charged at both entry and exit, turning +38 bp into
   −3.5 bp.

### Pillar 3 — the formula audit

`backtest/formula_audit.py` runs the **shipped** `compute_all` over real
minute tape and classifies every formula. Two kinds of silence are
distinguished, because they need opposite responses:

* **silent by design** (16 of 31) — the formula reads live state the replay
  cannot supply (GRC history rings, order book, forceOrder prints). Under
  the project's cause-6 honesty rule a missing book must not be invented.
  Deleting these would delete features.
* **dead** — constant output with no live dependency. **None remain.**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| alive | 15 | 15 |
| silent (live state, correct) | 16 | 16 |
| genuinely dead | 0 | 0 |
| near-inert (sd < 0.02, cannot vote) | **1 (F8)** | **0** |
| duplicate pairs (|corr| ≥ 0.85) | 0 | 0 |

**F8 RTDI was the one real find.** Its "tick density" was a
did-this-minute-change flag; on the native 1-minute tape **98.9 %** of bars
are distinct (measured 1.1 % identical), so the flag carried no information.
Output sd was **0.006** on 1h bars and **0.011** on 1m, against 0.3–0.9 for
every other formula: it could never move a consensus vote while looking
perfectly healthy to any non-dispersion check. Rewritten to measure volume
density around VWAP, sd rises to **0.455** (~40× the range).

### Bugs found in the monitors

1. **The decay detector killed both certified sleeves.** `DECAY_HALT_BPS =
   0.0` halted any sleeve whose first 20 trades averaged negative. With
   ~300 bp per-trade dispersion that happens to a +88 bp strategy by
   ordinary luck: it halted them at live `t` of −0.52 and −0.19. Zero is
   the wrong null against which to judge a certified edge.
2. **Peeking.** Re-testing a threshold on every new trade is reached
   eventually by any random walk. On the real catchup sleeve the z test
   first breached −2 at exactly n=88 — its *worst* running mean (−28.7 bp)
   — and the sleeve then recovered to **+38.0 bp** by n=165. Killing it there
   destroyed ~3500 bp of realized edge.
3. **A throttle that throttled nothing.** `throttled` was a status flag
   `size_for()` never read, so the correlation breaker and the de-risk path
   were decorative. Throttled sleeves now size at `THROTTLE_SIZE_SCALE`.
4. **Unbounded log growth.** Monitors run every bar and appended a reason
   each time, embedding numbers that change every bar; one replay produced
   hundreds of entries. Reasons are now recorded once per state change.
5. **`_channel_sign` was a mutable class attribute** shared by every
   `BrainSystem` in the process, so one calibration silently flipped a
   channel's sign for every brain constructed afterwards.

**Lesson worth keeping:** a monitor that deletes a certified edge is a worse
failure than the decay it was built to catch. The rule is now *de-risk now,
halt only on sustained contradiction*, judged against the certified number
rather than against zero.

### Test-suite hygiene

The suite was not repeatable: `test_engine.py`'s TP-ladder and SL tests
passed on a clean checkout and failed on a second run, with no code change.
The cause was `data/` — the engine loads learned state (`physics_weights`,
`auto_learning_state`, locks) from a **git-ignored** directory at start-up
and writes it back, so each run inherited the previous run's mutations.
`tests/conftest.py` now snapshots and restores `data/` for the session and
starts every run from the clean-clone state. Full suite: **515 passed**.

2. **PAXG** — nothing certified; 18 bp friction dominates the horizons

3. **Rolling re-certification** — the ~3500 bp swing above is the case for
